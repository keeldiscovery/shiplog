# Evidence plan

| Stakeholder | Why they matter | Evidence needed | Questions to ask | Assumption tested | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indie founder (solo, builds in public) | The target user — the one who would open ShipLog and read its draft | Confirmation the weekly write-up is a real recurring chore, and whether a machine-drafted first pass would actually be trusted | Q1, Q2, Q6 | A-001, A-004 | High |
| Indie founder (solo, builds in public) | Same segment, second independent voice — one person's habit isn't a pattern | Whether they'd ever skip reading a summary before it goes out; what a commit-only view of their week misses | Q3, Q4, Q5 | A-002, A-003 | High |
| Build-in-public reader | Validates the *output*, not just the drafting chore — if the update format isn't valued, faster drafting doesn't matter | What makes a weekly update worth reading vs. skipping | Q7 | A-004 | Medium |
| Indie founder (solo, builds in public) | Third independent voice on the two high-risk assumptions, to clear the evidence bar honestly rather than on two people | Same as above, weighted toward review-before-share behavior | Q3, Q4, Q8 | A-002, A-005 | Medium |

Notes:
- A-005 (comfort with public-API access) is low risk by design — a broken
  assumption here is a scoping detail (ask before connecting anything),
  not something that sinks the idea — so it's the lowest priority row and
  doesn't need dedicated interview time unless it comes up naturally.
- If early interviews show GitHub activity alone reads as "technically
  accurate but hollow" (missing the *why*), that's evidence against A-003,
  not evidence to throw out — it should shape what ships (e.g., a
  founder-editable context field) rather than being argued away.
